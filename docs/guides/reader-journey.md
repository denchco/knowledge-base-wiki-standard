# Shape the reader journey

Start with what the reader needs to understand or decide. Give a short answer to an agreed question, then make the detail available in stages. A well-linked source register cannot replace that explanation.

This proposed reusable advisory pattern illustrates the existing [writing policy](../spec/writing-style.md#reader-journey-guidance) under `DKBWS-PROMPT-001`. It preserves exact questions, evidence status and material qualifications. It adds no normative requirement, mandatory menu, semantic gate or automated readability score. [SRC-090](../sources.md#src-090)

## Establish the reader and question

Read the user's supplied material and existing Wiki first. Identify the primary reader, secondary readers and their decision or task. Use evidence rather than inventing audience preferences. If uncertainty would change the structure, ask one independent question and use the answer to choose the next.

A governing question defines what the Wiki must explain. Preserve its exact wording where the canonical-question contract applies. A subject-empty Wiki remains awaiting a seed; do not invent its audience facts, question, answer or evidence.

## Add depth in stages

| Layer | What the reader should gain | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| Short answer | The main conclusion and its practical consequence | A few substantive sentences, with conditions that change the result |
| Explanation | Why the answer holds and which distinctions matter | Connected prose, comparisons and terms explained at first need |
| Worked application | How the answer applies to a concrete situation | Actors, information or objects, triggers, actions, recipients and timing |
| Exact reference | The precise rule or record needed for a decision | Catalogues, detailed tables and clearly labelled authority or evidence status |
| Evidence and uncertainty | What supports the account and what remains unresolved | Exact source rows, evidence matrix, dates and validation queue |

These layers can share a page. Give separate pages distinct purposes. Add a glossary when repeated specialist terms justify one. Avoid repeating a full scope disclaimer on every page; place each necessary qualification where it affects the answer.

## Explain a worked application

For a subject-neutral example, consider a Wiki about a team's report-publication process. The example might identify a report owner, a completed report, an approval trigger, a publication action, the intended readers and an evidenced deadline. These are fields to explain, not invented facts or requirements about that team.

Describe the actual sequence before listing detailed exceptions. Make clear which facts are illustrative, which requirements come from a source and which conclusions depend on a condition. If the source does not establish an actor, recipient or deadline, record the gap rather than filling it.

A reader index can help readers find a long catalogue. It must not create extra obligations, count as additional records or silently change the classification of existing entries.

## Compare an opening with its evidence

This miniature example is fictional. It illustrates the method, not a real project rule. Assume the only source statement is: “The report owner publishes a report to the reader portal after recorded approval.”

For the question “How does an approved report reach its readers?”, compare:

| Opening | Review finding |
|---|---|
| “This Wiki contains our publication sources, methods and useful links. Start with the register.” | It describes the Wiki but leaves the question unanswered. |
| “The report owner puts approved reports on the reader portal. Publication follows recorded approval. The source gives no deadline.” | It answers the question, names the actor and route, preserves the approval condition and exposes the timing gap. |
| “All reports appear automatically within 24 hours.” | It invents universal coverage, automation and a deadline, and drops the approval condition. A shorter answer has changed the meaning. |

The second opening can lead to a worked sequence, then the exact reference. The comparison is a manual judgement; a successful build would not distinguish these meanings.

## Review the journey

For an entry-page or structure review, consider following one complete route from the short answer to exact evidence. A bounded review note can identify the pages, reviewer or method, date, findings and remaining uncertainty. An ordinary prose edit does not need a separate review record merely because this guide exists. Useful review questions include:

1. Does the opening answer the agreed question, including conditions that materially change the result?
2. Can the primary reader identify the practical consequence before opening a register?
3. Does each next layer add explanation rather than repeat the entry page?
4. Can a worked example be followed through its actors, actions and information?
5. Can the reader reach the exact reference and source without mistaking an index or example for authority?
6. Are unfamiliar terms explained when needed?
7. Are exact questions, source identities, force, scope, dates, exceptions and uncertainty preserved?
8. Does a subject-empty path avoid invented subject content?

Builds, links, source checks and policy-propagation fixtures remain necessary. They do not answer these semantic questions or prove comprehension. Record manual conclusions separately from automated results.
